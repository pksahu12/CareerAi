# **SECTION 1: REVERSE RECRUITING MARKET ANALYSIS**

## **1.1  What Reverse Recruiting Firms Actually Do**

Reverse recruiting firms act as outsourced job search agents for candidates — typically senior professionals earning $75K–$250K+. They provide a bundle of services that span document preparation, job discovery, application submission, networking outreach, and interview coaching. The core value proposition is simple: I am too busy or too overwhelmed to run a quality job search. Do it for me.

| Service Layer | What Firms Actually Do | Time Invested |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Resume & LinkedIn Optimization | Rewrite resume for ATS, optimize LinkedIn headline, about, and experience sections | 4–8 hrs (one-time) |
| Job Discovery & Curation | Manually search job boards (LinkedIn, Indeed, company sites) to build a target list | 3–5 hrs/week |
| Application Submission | Fill ATS forms on Workday, Greenhouse, Lever etc. using candidate data; tailor cover letters | 5–15 hrs/week |
| Hiring Manager Outreach | Find names on LinkedIn, craft personalised InMails and emails; follow up sequences | 3–5 hrs/week |
| Interview Preparation | Mock interviews, STAR framework coaching, company research briefs | 2–4 hrs per interview |
| Salary Negotiation | Market benchmarking, counter-offer scripting, negotiation coaching | 2–4 hrs total |
| Weekly Check-Ins | Progress calls, pipeline updates, strategy adjustments | 1–2 hrs/week |

## **1.3  Operational Bottlenecks in Traditional Firms**

Every traditional reverse recruiting firm shares the same fatal flaw: their service is fundamentally human-hours-dependent. Revenue can only grow by hiring more recruiters, each of whom can serve a limited number of clients simultaneously. This creates a ceiling on margin and scalability.

| ⚠️ RISK The core math: A human recruiter at $60K salary can manage 8–12 clients simultaneously at $2K–$3.5K/month each. Maximum revenue per recruiter \= \~$336K/year. After salary, benefits, tooling, and overhead, margin is 40–55%. This is a staffing agency, not a software company. |
| :---- |

### **Key Operational Bottlenecks**

* Application submission is manual data entry — copying resume fields into dozens of ATS forms — consuming 40–60% of recruiter time

* Personalization is inconsistent — cover letters and outreach messages vary in quality based on individual recruiter skill

* Job discovery is reactive — recruiters check the same job boards manually each day with no predictive intelligence

* Feedback loops are broken — firms rarely know why applications succeeded or failed, so they cannot improve systematically

* Client capacity is hard-capped — adding clients means adding headcount linearly

* Pricing is opaque and trust-dependent — candidates have no visibility into what actions were taken on their behalf

# **SECTION 2: MARKET GAPS & UNSERVED OPPORTUNITIES**

## **2.1  Top Pain Points Candidates Still Face**

* Price exclusivity: Quality reverse recruiting costs $3K–$15K, pricing out the majority of job seekers — the $40K–$100K segment is virtually unserved by premium services

* Black box processes: Candidates have zero visibility into what actions were taken, which applications were submitted, or why they received no responses

* One-size-fits-all applications: Even 'personalized' services send generic cover letters and identical resumes to every application

* Delayed feedback loops: It takes days or weeks for candidates to learn what's working — by which time the window has often closed

* No continuous learning: Each recruiter starts fresh — insights from 10,000 past applications aren't systematically applied to new candidates

* Interview prep is disconnected: Application and interview prep are separate products requiring separate purchases and separate vendors

* No real-time market intelligence: Candidates don't know which companies are hiring urgently, which are freezing headcount, or which roles have thin competition

* ISA trap: Income share agreements create anxiety and misaligned incentives — candidates want to negotiate up, but higher salary means higher ISA payment

## **3.3  AI Architecture & Workflows**

### **System Architecture Overview**

LaunchVector is built as an agentic AI system — not a tool that assists humans but a system of autonomous agents that execute end-to-end job search workflows on behalf of the candidate. The architecture follows a three-layer model:

| Layer | Components | Technology Stack |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Intelligence Layer (Brain) | Candidate profile model, Job-candidate matching engine, Outcome prediction model, Market intelligence signals | GPT-4o / Claude 3.5 Sonnet fine-tuned on domain data; custom embeddings; proprietary ranking models |
| Execution Layer (Hands) | Browser automation agent (ATS completion), LinkedIn outreach agent, Email drafting agent, Application tracker | Playwright/Puppeteer \+ custom ATS handlers; LinkedIn API \+ headless automation; Gmail/Outlook API |
| Experience Layer (Face) | Candidate dashboard, AI interview simulator, Resume editor, Real-time pipeline view, Voice coaching interface | Next.js \+ React; WebRTC voice; Vercel Edge; real-time WebSocket pipeline updates |

### **Core AI Workflows**

**Workflow 1: Autonomous Application Engine**

Candidate uploads resume → LaunchVector parses into structured profile → Semantic matching against live job postings → For each matched job: (a) tailor resume section-by-section to JD keywords, (b) generate targeted cover letter, (c) execute ATS form completion via browser agent, (d) record submission with timestamp and screenshot → Update application dashboard in real time.

**Workflow 2: Hiring Manager Outreach Agent**

For each submitted application → Identify hiring manager/team lead on LinkedIn → Enrich with company news (funding, launches, challenges), manager's recent posts, shared connections → Generate hyper-personalized InMail or email referencing specific and relevant context → A/B test message variants → Track open rates and reply rates → Feed response data back to personalization model.

**Workflow 3: Interview Intelligence Module**

Candidate receives interview invite → LaunchVector auto-generates company research brief (culture, recent news, likely questions from Glassdoor/Reddit/Blind data) → Schedules voice-AI mock interview session → AI interviewer asks role-specific questions in real-time → Real-time analysis of answer quality, clarity, confidence indicators, STAR framework adherence → Scoring report \+ improvement recommendations → Adaptive follow-up sessions targeting weaknesses.

**Workflow 4: Outcome Learning Loop**

Every application submitted creates a data record: {candidate\_profile, job\_description, resume\_version, cover\_letter, ATS\_platform, application\_date, response\_received, days\_to\_response, interview\_invited, offer\_made}. This dataset trains: (a) a job-matching ranker (which jobs are worth applying to for this profile), (b) a resume optimizer (which keyword combinations correlated with interviews), (c) an outreach scorer (which message structures generate replies). The system gets measurably smarter with every candidate it serves.

## **4.2  Defensibility Against Incumbents**

* Data moat: Every application submitted and every outcome recorded creates proprietary training data. After 1M applications, LaunchVector's match and prediction models are trained on data no competitor has access to. Incumbents (LinkedIn, Indeed) have job data but not application outcome data at the individual candidate level.

* ATS integration depth: Building reliable, accurate ATS form completion for 50+ platforms (Workday, Greenhouse, iCIMS, Taleo, Lever, Jobvite, etc.) requires thousands of hours of engineering per platform. This is a multi-year head start that creates a technical barrier.

* Network graph data: Over time, LaunchVector accumulates a proprietary graph of which connections led to which warm introductions and interview conversions. This relationship intelligence compounds in value with scale.

* Brand trust in high-stakes category: Job search is deeply personal and emotionally charged. A brand that delivers results builds emotional loyalty that is extremely difficult for competitors to dislodge. First mover \+ strong outcomes \= category-defining brand.

* Switching costs: Candidates who have trained LaunchVector on their preferences, resume history, and application data have strong reasons to stay — recreating that context elsewhere is friction.

## **5.5  Lean Validation Plan**

Before spending engineering time building features, validate each assumption with the lowest possible cost experiment:

* Assumption 1 — People will pay $149/mo for AI apply: Run a landing page with Stripe checkout BEFORE building the product. If 10/100 visitors convert (10%), demand is real. Target: 100 sign-ups in 2 weeks from personal network \+ LinkedIn posts.

* Assumption 2 — AI applications generate interviews: Track interview rate for first 50 beta users. Target: \>15% of quality applications receiving responses. Benchmark against industry average of 5–8%.

* Assumption 3 — Hiring manager outreach improves conversion: A/B test: candidates who receive outreach \+ application vs. application alone. Measure interview rate delta. Target: 2x conversion rate improvement.

* Assumption 4 — Enterprises will pay for outplacement: Conduct 20 CHRO/VP People interviews asking: 'Would you pay $2K per employee to guarantee they land a job within 60 days?' Target: 30%+ say yes with budget authority.

* Assumption 5 — Coaches want white-label API: Offer 10 coaches free API access for 60 days. Track adoption, client results shared, willingness to pay $199/seat/month.
